## Experian — Global Corporate Rebrand and Operating Model
Experian needed to launch a global corporate identity but did not yet have a clear global web operating model. Michael joined initially as a contractor and helped build standardized shell sites containing core corporate information across roughly 40 countries and 19 languages.

Guiding principles:
1. Do no harm.
2. Implement incrementally.

Michael identified local marketers across North America, UK/IE, EMEA, LATAM, and APAC and established recurring meetings. He trained regional leads, who then trained their local teams. Over time, the model expanded beyond content publishing into analytics, SEO, lead generation, and B2B sales reporting.

The rollout started in lower-risk, lower-traffic regions before reaching more complex and higher-traffic markets. The purpose was to learn at smaller scale before increasing business risk.

Lessons:
- Global standards work best when paired with local ownership.
- Train-the-trainer models create durable scale.
- Incremental rollout is a risk-management strategy, not a lack of ambition.
- Web operations can create commercial value even when the function is treated as a cost center.

## Meta — Replacing Spreadsheet-Based Tracking
A Meta-related project was being tracked in Excel, creating overlapping edits, weak ownership, administrative effort, and limited visibility. Michael recommended moving to task-management software with assignments, notifications, and progress tracking.

Results included clearer accountability, reduced confusion, better progress visibility, and time and cost savings.

Lessons:
- Automation should remove coordination overhead, not human judgment.
- The right operational tool makes ownership and dependencies visible.
- Product operations often starts with fixing how work moves.

## Monster.com — International Migration Acceleration
After moving to Temecula, Michael began attending local technology meetups. At a developer meetup, he joined a collaborative project by contributing project-management structure: creating tasks, prioritizing work, and sequencing dependencies.

That relationship led to a six-month engagement with TE Web Solutions on an international Monster.com job-seeker migration. The program had completed one migration in the previous six months. Michael introduced Agile practices and two-week sprints, helping the team complete the remaining country migrations over the next six months.

The LinkedIn profile describes more than 15 international migrations and more than 20,000 pages. The exact site count should be confirmed before using it publicly.

Lessons:
- You do not need to write the code to increase a technical team's delivery velocity.
- Visible, sequenced, finishable work can be a high-leverage technical contribution.
- Product operations existed as a practice before the title became common.
- Community participation can create meaningful professional opportunities.
